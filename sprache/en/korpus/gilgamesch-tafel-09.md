For Enkidu, his friend, Gilgamesh wept
bitterly as he roamed the wild:
(Gilgamesh:) "I shall die, and shall I not then be as Enkidu?
Grief is gone into my heart.
Death I fear, therefore I roam the wild,
to Ūta-napišti, the son of Ubār-Tutu,
I am upon the road and hasten on.
I came by night unto the passes of the mountains,
I saw lions and was afraid.
I lifted my head and prayed unto Sîn,
unto the light of the gods went my prayers:
'Sîn, keep me whole!'"
Gilgamesh started up from sleep, it was a dream!
Before the face of the moon he rejoiced to be alive.
He took his axe in his hand,
he drew the dagger from his belt,
like an arrow he fell among them,
he smote the lions, slew them and scattered them.

The name of the mountain was Māšu.
To the twin mountain Māšu he came,
that day by day guardeth the rising of the sun,
its peaks touch the vault of heaven,
its foot reacheth down into the netherworld.
Scorpion men guarded its gate,
whose terror was dread and whose glance was death,
whose radiance was fearful and enfolded the mountains,
at the rising and the setting they guard the sun.
Gilgamesh saw them and veiled his face for fear and dread,
then he gathered himself and stepped before them.
The scorpion man called unto his wife:
(Scorpion man:) "He that is come unto us, his body is flesh of the gods."
The wife of the scorpion man answered him:
(Scorpion woman:) "Two thirds of him are god, one third of him is man."
The scorpion man called out
and spake a word unto King Gilgamesh, the flesh of the gods:
(Scorpion man:) "How camest thou hither, a far road,
how hast thou come hither before my face?
How didst thou cross the many rivers whose crossing is perilous?
Whither thy face is set, that let me know."
(Gilgamesh:) "I seek the road of my forefather Ūta-napišti,
who stood in the assembly of the gods and found life,
of death and life he shall tell me the secret."
The scorpion man opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Scorpion man:) "Never was there, Gilgamesh, one like thee,
never did any go the path through the mountain.
Twelve double hours its inside reacheth,
dense is the darkness, and no light is there."
(Gilgamesh:) "Through grief, through frost and the glare of the sun my face is burnt,
through weariness I am spent."
The scorpion man opened his mouth
and spake a word unto King Gilgamesh, the flesh of the gods:
(Scorpion man:) "Go, Gilgamesh!
Let the mountains of Māšu let thee pass,
let the mountains and hills watch over thy going,
let them help thee to go on thy road in safety,
let the gate of the mountain open before thee!"

When Gilgamesh heard this,
he took at the word of the scorpion man the road of the sun.
One double hour he went,
dense was the darkness, and no light was there,
it let him not see what was behind him.
Two double hours, three, four and five,
six, seven and eight double hours, he hastened on,
dense was the darkness, and no light was there,
it let him not see what was behind him.
Nine double hours, then he felt the north wind,
it brushed across his face,
dense was the darkness, and no light was there.
When he reached ten double hours, it was very near,
after eleven double hours there remained one double hour of road,
after twelve double hours Gilgamesh came forth before the sun.

There was radiance,
and as soon as he saw them he went straight unto the trees of the gods.
A tree of carnelian bore fruit,
hung with grapes, lovely to behold.
A tree of lapis lazuli bore leaves,
full of fruit and glorious to see.
There were cypress and cedar,
their leaf stalks of pappardilû stone,
sea coral and sāsu stone,
instead of thorn and thistle grew little flasks of crystal.
He touched a carob tree, it was of abašmu stone,
of šubû stone and bloodstone,
like turquoise it shone,
and shells of the sea hung upon it.
As Gilgamesh walked about in wonder,
a woman lifted her head and watched him.
