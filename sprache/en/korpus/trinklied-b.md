(Guests:) "If the Gakkul standeth on its rack, so standeth the Lamsare,
if the Lamsare standeth, soon we shall sway,
Gakkul, thou vat that hummeth like a beehive,
Lamsare, thou jug that sweateth like a bridegroom,
Ugurbala, thou chest full of thirst,
Šagube and Amam, ye servants who never rest,
Buniĝ and Bandudu, ye scoopers and carriers,
all of you stand ready like an army before the battle,
and may the heart of thy god, master of the house, grumble no more!
Draw from the Gakkul, and the god falleth silent,
draw once more, and the neighbours fall silent too.
Our liver is glad, our heart is merry,
and what befalleth this night, Ninkasi hath seen it and will tell no one.
Upon thy threshold is water sprinkled, thy bricks are blessed,
so may the beer woman sleep beneath thy roof,
may she bring jugs before thou callest,
and jugs when thou canst call no more!"

(Master of the house:) "I walk around the vat and draw,
the boy with the jug followeth me like a little dog,
I drink, and I am wondrous well,
I drink again, and my liver singeth,
I fall silent with bliss and then say too much,
I wrap my heart in purple and lead it out like a bride,
and so, verily, the heart of Inana is appeased!"

(Woman:) "Appeased she is. By thee, master of the house, not."
