Whoever thou art, O witch, that hast drawn clay of me from the river,
that hast buried my images in a darksome house,
that hast hidden my water in a grave,
that hast gathered my leavings from the rubbish pit,
that hast cut off my hem in the house of the washer,
that hast swept the dust of my feet from the threshold:
I have sent to the gate of the quay, they have bought me tallow for thine image,
I have sent to the ditch of the city, they have pinched off clay for thine image.
Against thee I send the blazing oven, the burning Girra,
Girra that ever reneweth himself, the abiding light of the gods,
Sîn from Ur, Šamaš from Larsa,
Nergal with his hosts,
Ištar of Akkade with her sanctuary,
to gather the seed of my warlock and my witch, as much as there is.
Let them slay the witch, but let me live,
for I bewitched her not, but she bewitched me,
I lay not in wait for her, but she lay in wait for me.
She trusteth in her cunning witchcraft,
but I in the abiding light of the gods, in Girra, the judge.
Girra, burn her, Girra, scorch her,
Girra, vanquish her!
