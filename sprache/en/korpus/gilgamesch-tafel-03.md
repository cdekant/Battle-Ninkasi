(Elders:) "Come back whole to the quay of Uruk!
Trust not, Gilgamesh, in the fullness of thy strength,
let thine eyes look their fill, strike a blow thou canst build upon!
He that goeth before saveth his comrade,
he that knoweth the path guardeth his friend.
Let Enkidu go before thee,
he knoweth the way to the Cedar Forest,
he hath seen the battle and known the fight.
Let Enkidu guard his friend and keep his comrade whole,
let him bring him back in his body unto his wives!
In our assembly we entrust the king unto thee,
thou shalt bring him back and entrust the king again unto us."

Gilgamesh opened his mouth and spake unto Enkidu:
(Gilgamesh:) "Come, my friend, let us go to the Egalmaḫ,
before the face of Ninsun, the great queen.
Ninsun is clever and wise, she knoweth all things,
steps of counsel will she lay before our feet."
They took each other, hand in hand,
Gilgamesh and Enkidu went to the Egalmaḫ,
before the face of Ninsun, the great queen.
Gilgamesh arose and went in before the goddess, his mother,
Gilgamesh spake unto her, unto Ninsun:
(Gilgamesh:) "Ninsun, I am grown so bold as to go the far road
thither where Ḫumbaba is.
A battle I know not shall I face,
a path I know not shall I ride.
I beseech thee, bless me, that I may go,
that I may see thy face again in safety
and enter glad of heart through the gate of Uruk.
I will come home and hold the Akītu feast twice in the year,
hold will I the Akītu twice in the year.
Let the Akītu be kept and the rejoicing begin,
let the drums roar before thy face!"

The Wild Cow Ninsun heard full of sorrow
the words of Gilgamesh, her son, and of Enkidu.
Seven times she went into the bath house,
she cleansed herself with water of tamarisk and soapwort,
she put on a fine garment, the adornment of her body,
the adornment of her breast,
and set her crown upon her head.
She sprang up the stair, she climbed upon the roof,
she climbed upon the roof and set a censer before Šamaš,
she strewed incense before Šamaš and lifted up her arms:
(Ninsun:) "Why didst thou give my son Gilgamesh a heart that sleepeth not, and lay it upon him?
Now thou hast touched him, and he goeth
the far road thither where Ḫumbaba is.
A battle he knoweth not shall he face,
a path he knoweth not shall he ride.
So long as he goeth and returneth,
until he reacheth the Cedar Forest,
until he slayeth Ḫumbaba, the raging one,
and blotteth out of the land all evil that thou hatest,
may Aya, the bride, not shrink from thee, but remind thee:
'Entrust him unto the watches of the night!'"

(Ninsun:) "Thou hast opened, Šamaš, the going out unto the herds,
thou camest forth over the land,
the mountains and the heavens grow bright,
the herds of the wild wait upon thy ruddy light,
at thy coming forth the multitudes gather,
the Anunnaki wait intent upon thy light.
May Aya, the bride, not shrink from thee, but remind thee:
'Entrust him unto the watches of the night!
Lay for his feet a road of safety,
touch Enkidu, that he go before him!
Because he knoweth the way to the Cedar Forest,
let him clear the path and make the mountains passable,
a torch for the journey let him bear in his hand.
So long as Gilgamesh goeth to the Cedar Forest,
let the days be long and the nights be short,
girt be his loins, wide his stride!
At dusk let him pitch a camp for the night.'
May Aya, the great bride, not shrink from thee, but remind thee!"

(Ninsun:) "On the day that Gilgamesh, Enkidu and Ḫumbaba stand face to face,
rouse, Šamaš, against Ḫumbaba the great storms:
South Wind, North Wind, East Wind, West Wind, gust and countergust,
weather and foul weather, whirlwind and wind of doom,
ice wind, hurricane and waterspout:
let thirteen winds arise, that the face of Ḫumbaba be darkened
and the weapon of Gilgamesh reach Ḫumbaba!
When thine own heart is come to rest,
then, Šamaš, turn thy face unto thy mules,
thy swift mules shall bring thee home betimes,
a quiet seat, a bed for the night be made ready for thee,
the gods thy brothers shall bring thee food to gladden thee,
Aya, the great bride, wipe thy face with the clean hem of her garment!"

Again the Wild Cow Ninsun laid her desire before Šamaš:
(Ninsun:) "Šamaš, shall not Gilgamesh be counted among the gods?
Shall he not share the heavens with thee?
Shall he not share the sceptre with Sîn?
Shall he not grow wise with Ea in the Deep?
Shall he not rule with Irnina over the black-headed folk?
Shall he not dwell with Ningišzida in the land of no return?"

When the Wild Cow Ninsun had brought her desire before Šamaš,
the Wild Cow Ninsun, clever and wise, who knoweth all things,
she smothered the censer and came down from the roof.
She called Enkidu and made known to him her will:
(Ninsun:) "Mighty Enkidu, thou art not the fruit of my womb,
yet now let thy brood be with the votaries of Gilgamesh,
with the priestesses, the consecrated and the temple maidens."
She laid the tokens about Enkidu's neck:
(Ninsun:) "The priestesses took in the foundling,
the daughters of the gods rear the foster child.
I myself take Enkidu, whom I love, for my son,
let Gilgamesh do good to Enkidu in brotherhood!
So long as ye go to the Cedar Forest,
let the days be long and the nights be short,
girt be your loins, wide your stride,
at dusk pitch a camp for the night!"

(Gilgamesh:) "So long as we go and return,
until we reach the Cedar Forest,
until we slay Ḫumbaba, the raging one,
and blot out of the land all evil that Šamaš hateth,
let not the young men gather in the lane.
Judge the cause of the weak, seek out his right,
until like little children we gain what we long for,
until we plant our weapons at the gate of Ḫumbaba!"

The commanders stood there and blessed him,
the young men of Uruk ran in throngs behind him,
and the commanders kissed his feet:
(Commanders:) "Come back whole to the quay of Uruk!
Trust not, Gilgamesh, in the fullness of thy strength,
let thine eyes look their fill, strike a blow thou canst build upon!
He that goeth before saveth his comrade,
he that knoweth the path shall guard his friend.
Let Enkidu go before thee,
he knoweth the way to the Cedar Forest,
he hath seen the battle and known the fight.
Let Enkidu guard his friend and keep his comrade whole,
let him bring him back in his body unto his wives!
In our assembly we entrust the king unto thee,
thou shalt bring him back and entrust the king again unto us."
