(Palm:) "I am the date palm,
tall am I of stature,
fair sitteth my crown upon me as upon the cypress.
At my feet the shepherd and the shepherd boy, his son, rest with their flock,
the cows rest with their calves at my feet.
In my sweet shade the stag seeketh refuge,
for the host and the camp I ease their weariness.
Over my luscious fruit mankind rejoiceth,
richly do I spread the table of the gods and bring them the full offerings.
Without me the exorcist speaketh no spell,
with my work he accomplisheth every washing of hands.
Like the grain goddess I deal out sustenance unto the land,
fine beer, the life of mankind, I bestow upon all dwellings.
Through my gifts the whole body of mankind groweth tall,
the farmer bringeth the team unto his plough.
From my pith the boatman finisheth his barge,
the weaver maketh fast therewith all her gear.
Rope is the work of my fronds:
the fisherman taketh meshes for his net,
the fowler plaiteth threads for his snare,
the builder raiseth the ruins again with my beams.
The priest adorneth with my pith his offering of flour,
the miller bringeth his portion unto the table of gods and king.
In the gate, the entrance of the gods, my doors stretch wide,
my name, King, surpasseth that of all other trees.
Wherewith wilt thou measure thyself against me in strife,
that thou comest boldly against me and pickest a quarrel?"

The vine heard the speech of the palm,
it opened its mouth and spake unto it:
(Vine:) "Quarrelsome slanderer, come, I will tell thee somewhat!
Why revilest thou me without thought? I will strive with thee.
I am the vine that cleanseth all things, the drink of the mountains!
In the cup of Šamaš, at the table of the gods, I mix the tithe,
and Šamaš, the bond of mankind, the sun of the people,
his heart I widen, his face I make to shine.
Clad in his finery he calleth for a joyful song
and returneth to the holy precinct and showeth mercy.
After my name, Karānu, they call every draught Kurunnu,
for all the black-headed folk give heed to me wholly.
I heal the sick man whom the fever seizeth,
I bring back the ailing and the dying from the netherworld.
He from whose breast life hath ebbed away,
whom death hath covered and beareth to the grave,
on his deathbed I spare his life!
I sweeten the singer of laments that wailed for him,
that mourned for him without ceasing and bound his arms:
through me he must give way, and his captive goeth free.
Whoso beholdeth me by night, it goeth well with him,
he marcheth at the head of the people and standeth in the foremost rank.
Now, since thou hast said: I am glorious of form,
fair sitteth my crown and luscious is my fruit,
know this: the almond tree that lifteth its boughs at the head of the ditch
surpasseth the poplar that lieth in its ditch."
