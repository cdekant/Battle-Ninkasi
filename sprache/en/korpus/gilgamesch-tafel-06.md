He washed his matted hair, he cleaned his gear,
he shook his locks down over his back.
He cast off what was soiled and clothed himself in what was clean,
wrapped himself in cloaks and bound them with the sash.
Gilgamesh set his crown upon his head.
Then the lady Ištar cast an eye upon the beauty of Gilgamesh:
(Ištar:) "Come, Gilgamesh, be thou my bridegroom!
Give me thy fruit, so I will have it!
Thou shalt be my husband, and I will be thy wife!
A chariot of lapis lazuli and gold will I harness for thee,
its wheels of gold, its horns of amber,
storm lions shalt thou yoke, mighty mules.
Enter into our house in the fragrance of cedar!
When thou enterest into our house,
threshold and throne shall kiss thy feet,
kings, courtiers and princes shall bow beneath thee,
the yield of mountain and plain they shall bring thee as tribute!
Thy goats shall bear triplets, thy ewes twins,
thy ass foal under its load shall outrun the mule,
at the chariot thy steed shall gallop in splendour,
beneath the yoke thine ox shall find no equal."

Gilgamesh opened his mouth and spake unto the lady Ištar:
(Gilgamesh:) "If I should take thee to wife,
whence should come my body and my garment,
whence my food and my sustenance?
Wilt thou give me bread that befitteth a god,
wilt thou give me fine beer to drink, the ornament of kingship?
Why should I take thee to wife?
Thou art a hoarfrost that bindeth no ice,
a door of laths that keepeth out neither wind nor draught,
a palace that crusheth the heroes,
an elephant that casteth off its cover,
pitch that fouleth the hand of its bearer,
a waterskin that drencheth its bearer,
a boulder that breaketh the wall of stone,
a battering ram that wrecketh the wall of the enemy land,
a shoe that biteth its master's foot!
Which of thy bridegrooms endured for ever?
Which of thy heroes went up unto heaven?
Come, let me reckon thee the tally of thy lovers.

To Dumuzi, the husband of thy youth,
thou hast decreed everlasting weeping, year upon year.
Thou didst love the speckled allallu bird,
thou didst smite him and break his wing,
now he standeth in the woods and crieth: 'My wing!'
Thou didst love the lion, perfect in strength,
seven and seven pits hast thou dug for him.
Thou didst love the steed, renowned in battle,
whip, spur and lash hast thou decreed him,
seven leagues of galloping hast thou decreed him,
troubled water to drink hast thou decreed him,
to his mother Silili thou hast decreed everlasting weeping.
Thou didst love the shepherd, the keeper, the herdsman,
who ever heaped up for thee bread baked in the ashes
and slaughtered kids for thee day by day,
thou didst smite him and turn him into a wolf,
now his own shepherd boys hunt him,
and his dogs bite at his thighs.

Thou didst love Išullānu, the gardener of thy father,
who ever brought thee a basket of dates
and made thy table shine day by day,
thou didst cast an eye upon him and go unto him:
'My Išullānu, let us taste thy strength,
put forth thy hand and touch our lap!'
Išullānu spake unto thee:
'I? What dost thou ask of me?
Did my mother not bake, have I not eaten?
Shall I now eat the bread of shame and curses,
shall rushes be my covering in the frost?'
Thou didst hear this his speech,
thou didst smite him and turn him into a dwarf,
thou didst set him in the midst of his toil,
he cannot go up, he cannot go down.
And me thou wouldst love and do unto me as unto them?"

When Ištar heard this,
Ištar was wroth and went up unto heaven.
Weeping Ištar went before Anu, her father,
before Antu, her mother, her tears did flow:
(Ištar:) "Father, Gilgamesh hath heaped insults upon me,
Gilgamesh hath reckoned me slur upon slur,
slur and shame upon me."
Anu opened his mouth and spake unto the lady Ištar:
(Anu:) "Well now, wast thou not she that provoked King Gilgamesh,
so that Gilgamesh reckoned thee slur upon slur,
slur and shame upon thee?"
Ištar opened her mouth and spake unto Anu, her father:
(Ištar:) "Father, give me, I pray thee, the Bull of Heaven,
that I may slay Gilgamesh in his dwelling!
If thou give me not the Bull of Heaven,
I will smash the netherworld with its dwelling,
to the world below I will grant release,
I will raise up the dead, that they devour the living,
more than the living will I make the dead!"
Anu opened his mouth and spake unto the lady Ištar:
(Anu:) "If thou ask of me the Bull of Heaven,
then for seven years shall the widow of Uruk glean chaff
and the farmer of Uruk grow hay."
Ištar opened her mouth and spake unto Anu, her father:
(Ištar:) "For seven years hath the widow of Uruk gleaned chaff,
the farmer of Uruk hath grown hay.
With the wrath of the Bull will I be avenged!"
Anu heard this speech of Ištar
and laid in her hands the nose rope of the Bull of Heaven.

Ištar came down and led it onward.
When it reached the land of Uruk,
it parched the woodland, the marsh and the reeds,
it went down to the river, and the river sank by seven cubits.
At the snort of the Bull of Heaven a pit opened,
a hundred men of Uruk fell into it.
At its second snort a pit opened,
two hundred men of Uruk fell into it.
At its third snort a pit opened,
Enkidu fell into it up to his hips.
Enkidu sprang out and seized the Bull of Heaven by the horns,
the Bull of Heaven spat slaver in his face
and flung its dung with the tuft of its tail.
Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, we have vaunted ourselves in our city,
how shall we answer for the people heaped up?
My friend, I have tried the might of the Bull of Heaven
and know its strength and its intent.
Once more will I try the might of the Bull of Heaven,
I will step behind the Bull of Heaven
and seize it by the tuft of its tail,
I will set my foot in the bend of its hock.
Then shalt thou like a butcher, bold and skilful,
thrust thy knife between the yoke of the horns and the slaughtering place!"
Enkidu went round behind the Bull of Heaven,
he seized it by the tuft of its tail,
he set his foot in the bend of its hock,
and Gilgamesh like a butcher, bold and skilful,
thrust his knife between the yoke of the horns and the slaughtering place.

When they had slain the Bull of Heaven,
they took its heart and set it before Šamaš.
They stepped back and cast themselves down before Šamaš,
then the two of them sat down together.
Ištar went up upon the wall of Uruk the Fold,
she leapt and stamped, she uttered a cry of woe:
(Ištar:) "Woe unto Gilgamesh, who reviled me and slew the Bull of Heaven!"
Enkidu heard this speech of Ištar,
he tore a haunch from the Bull of Heaven and flung it down before her:
(Enkidu:) "Thee also, had I caught thee, I would have done unto thee as unto it,
its guts would I have hung about thine arms!"
Ištar gathered the votaries, the girls of pleasure and the harlots,
over the haunch of the Bull of Heaven she held the lament for the dead.

Gilgamesh called the craftsmen, all the smiths,
and the craftsmen praised the thickness of its horns:
thirty minas of lapis lazuli weighed each one,
two minas each rim,
six kor of oil they held together.
He dedicated them for the anointing of his god Lugalbanda,
he bore them in and hung them in his bedchamber.
They washed their hands in the Euphrates,
they took each other and went onward.
As they drove through the lane of Uruk,
the folk of Uruk were gathered to behold them.
Gilgamesh spake a word unto the maids of his house:
(Gilgamesh:) "Who is the most glorious among men?
Who is the most splendid among the lads?"
(Maids:) "Gilgamesh is the most glorious among men,
Gilgamesh is the most splendid among the lads!"
Gilgamesh held a feast of joy in his palace.
Then the men lay down that slept upon their beds by night,
and Enkidu also lay down and saw a dream.
Enkidu arose to reveal the dream
and spake unto his friend.
