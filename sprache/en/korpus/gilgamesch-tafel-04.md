At twenty leagues they broke bread,
at thirty leagues they pitched the night camp.
Fifty leagues they went in a day,
a road of a month and a half on the third day, they drew nigh to Mount Lebanon.
Facing the sun they dug a well,
fresh water they put into their skins.
Gilgamesh climbed to the top of the mountain,
his offering of flour he poured out to the hills:
(Gilgamesh:) "Mountain, bring me a dream, let me see good tidings from Šamaš!"
Enkidu built him a house for the dream spirit,
he set a door against the storm in its entrance,
in the circle he had drawn he made him lie,
and he himself lay flat as a net in the door.
Gilgamesh rested his chin upon his knees,
the sleep that poureth over mankind fell upon him.
In the middle watch his sleep came to its end,
he arose and spake unto his friend:
(Gilgamesh:) "My friend, didst thou not call me? Why am I awake?
Didst thou not touch me? Why am I troubled?
Did not a god pass by? Why is my flesh so numb?
My friend, I saw a first dream,
and the dream that I saw was wholly confused:
upon the slope of a mountain we stood,
the mountain fell down between us,
but we flew over it like flies."
He that was born in the wild knew counsel,
Enkidu spake unto his friend and read him the dream:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, good is thy dream,
precious is the dream and boded us well.
My friend, the mountain thou sawest is Ḫumbaba,
we shall seize Ḫumbaba and slay him,
his corpse we shall cast upon the field of battle.
And in the morning we shall see good tidings from Šamaš."

At twenty leagues they broke bread, at thirty leagues they pitched the night camp,
they dug a well, and Gilgamesh climbed the mountain:
(Gilgamesh:) "Mountain, bring me a dream!"
Enkidu built him the dream house, and sleep fell upon Gilgamesh.
In the middle watch he arose and spake unto his friend:
(Gilgamesh:) "My friend, I saw a second dream,
and the dream that I saw was wholly confused:
with my shoulder I propped up a mountain,
the mountain fell upon me and pressed me down.
Weakness closed about my legs,
a blazing radiance overcame my arms.
There was a man, like a lion he came unto me,
the brightest in the land, the fairest of form,
he took me by the arm
and drew me out from under the mountain."
Enkidu read the dream and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, he to whom we go,
is he not the mountain? He is a thing strange!
Thou and he shall stand face to face, and thou shalt do a deed without peer,
the custom of the warrior, the work of a man.
He will let his fury rage against thee,
the fear of him will close about thy legs.
But he whom thou sawest was Šamaš, the king,
in thy need he will take thee by the hand.
And in the morning we shall see good tidings from Šamaš."

At twenty leagues they broke bread, at thirty leagues they pitched the night camp,
they dug a well, and Gilgamesh climbed the mountain:
(Gilgamesh:) "Mountain, bring me a dream!"
Enkidu built him the dream house, and sleep fell upon Gilgamesh.
In the middle watch he arose and spake unto his friend:
(Gilgamesh:) "My friend, I saw a third dream,
and the dream that I saw was wholly confused:
the heavens cried out, the earth roared,
the day grew still, the darkness came forth.
Lightning flashed, a fire blazed up,
the flames leapt, and death rained down.
Then the bright glare grew dim, the fire went out,
the flames sank down and turned to ash.
Thou wast born in the wild, can we find counsel?"
Enkidu heard the words of his friend, he read him the dream and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, good is thy dream, and its tidings promise fortune."

At twenty leagues they broke bread, at thirty leagues they pitched the night camp,
they dug a well, and Gilgamesh climbed the mountain:
(Gilgamesh:) "Mountain, bring me a dream!"
Enkidu built him the dream house, and sleep fell upon Gilgamesh.
In the middle watch he arose and spake unto his friend:
(Gilgamesh:) "My friend, I saw a fourth dream,
and the dream that I saw was wholly confused."
(Enkidu:) "My friend, good is thy dream,
fire shall be kindled upon Ḫumbaba,
we shall throw him down and bind his arms,
we shall stand upon him.
And in the morning we shall see good tidings from Šamaš."

At twenty leagues they broke bread, at thirty leagues they pitched the night camp,
they dug a well, and Gilgamesh climbed the mountain:
(Gilgamesh:) "Mountain, bring me a dream!"
Enkidu built him the dream house, and sleep fell upon Gilgamesh.
In the middle watch he arose and spake unto his friend:
(Gilgamesh:) "My friend, I saw a fifth dream,
and the dream that I saw was wholly confused."
(Enkidu:) "The man thou sawest shall seize him by the wings."

Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, he to whom we go is a thing strange,
Ḫumbaba, to whom we go, is a thing strange!"
