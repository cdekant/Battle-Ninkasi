I call upon you, ye gods of the night,
with you I call upon the night, the veiled bride,
I call upon the evening, the midnight and the dawn.
For a witch hath bewitched me,
a schemer hath accused me,
my god and my goddess hath she driven from me,
whoso looketh upon me, to him I am grown loathsome,
I find no rest by night nor by day.
A gag filleth my mouth without ceasing,
bread it keepeth far from my mouth,
the water of my drinking it hath lessened,
my song of joy is become wailing, my gladness a lament for the dead.

Stand by me, ye great gods, hear my plea,
judge my cause, decide my way!
An image of my warlock and my witch have I made,
of my sorcerer and of her that stirreth up the sorcery,
I lay it at your feet and plead my cause:
because she did evil and sought what is not good,
let her die, but let me live!
Let her witchcraft, her spittle, her bonds be loosed!
Let the tamarisk with the rich crown cleanse me,
let the date palm that withstandeth every wind release me,
let the soapwort that filleth the earth wash me,
let the cone full of seed release me.
Before you I am become pure as the grass,
clean and clear as the nard.

The spell of the evil witch
returneth into her mouth, her tongue is bound.
For her witchcraft let the gods of the night smite her,
let the three watches of the night loose her evil spittle.
Her mouth be tallow, her tongue be salt:
the mouth that spake the evil word against me, let it melt away like tallow,
the tongue that worked the witchcraft, let it dissolve like salt.
Her knots are loosed, her work is brought to nought,
all her words fill the steppe,
at the word that the gods of the night have spoken.
