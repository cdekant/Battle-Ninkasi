(Gilgamesh:) "Would that I had left the ball today in the carpenter's workshop!
Wife of the carpenter, who art to me as the mother that bore me, would that I had left it!
Daughter of the carpenter, who art to me as my little sister, would that I had left it!
Today the ball is fallen into the netherworld,
my mallet is fallen into the netherworld!"
Enkidu answered Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My lord, why weepest thou, why is thy heart troubled?
Today will I myself bring thee up the ball from the netherworld,
I myself will bring thee up thy mallet from the netherworld!"
Gilgamesh answered Enkidu:
(Gilgamesh:) "If thou goest down into the netherworld,
then heed my bidding!
Put on no clean garment,
else they will know thee for a stranger!
Anoint thyself not with sweet oil from the jar,
else at its scent they will gather about thee!
Hurl no throwstick in the netherworld,
else they that were struck by the throwstick will surround thee!
Bear no staff in thy hand,
else the shades will tremble before thee!
Put no sandals upon thy feet,
make no noise in the netherworld!
Kiss not the wife thou lovest,
strike not the wife thou hatest,
kiss not the son thou lovest,
strike not the son thou hatest,
else the outcry of the netherworld will seize thee!
She that lieth, she that lieth, the mother of Ninazu that lieth,
her gleaming shoulders are covered by no garment,
her breast, laid bare, is like a jar of stone."

Enkidu went down into the netherworld
and heeded not the bidding of Gilgamesh:
he put on a clean garment, and they knew him for a stranger,
he anointed himself with sweet oil, and at its scent they gathered about him,
he hurled the throwstick, and the stricken surrounded him,
he bore a staff, and the shades trembled,
he wore sandals and made noise in the netherworld,
he kissed the wife he loved, he struck the wife he hated,
he kissed the son he loved, he struck the son he hated,
and the outcry of the netherworld seized him.
Then Enkidu came not up again from the netherworld to the world above.
Namtar seized him not, the Wasting seized him not, the netherworld seized him!
The pitiless constable of Nergal seized him not, the netherworld seized him!
He fell not where men do battle, the netherworld seized him!

Then the king, the son of Ninsun, went weeping for his servant Enkidu,
alone he went to Ekur, the house of Enlil:
(Gilgamesh:) "Father Enlil, today the ball is fallen into the netherworld,
my mallet is fallen into the netherworld!
Enkidu, who went down to bring them up, the netherworld hath seized!
Namtar seized him not, the Wasting seized him not, the netherworld seized him!
He fell not where men do battle, the netherworld seized him!"
Father Enlil answered him not a word.
He went to Ur, to the house of Sîn, and spake likewise,
Father Sîn answered him not a word.
He went to Eridu, to the house of Ea, and spake likewise,
Father Ea helped him in this matter,
he spake unto the young hero Šamaš:
(Ea:) "Young hero Šamaš, son of Ningal,
open, I pray thee, a crack in the netherworld
and bring up the shade of Enkidu like a breath from the netherworld!"
The young hero Šamaš, the son of Ningal,
opened a crack in the netherworld
and brought up the shade of Enkidu like a breath from the netherworld.

They embraced each other and kissed,
they exchanged thoughts and questions:
(Gilgamesh:) "Tell me, my friend, tell me, my friend,
tell me the order of the netherworld that thou hast seen!"
(Enkidu:) "I cannot tell thee, my friend, I cannot tell thee!
If I tell thee the order of the netherworld that I have seen,
then sit thee down and weep!"
(Gilgamesh:) "Then let me sit down and weep!"
(Enkidu:) "My friend, the member that thou touchedst, so that thy heart rejoiced,
the worms devour like an old beam.
My friend, the lap that thou didst feel, so that thy heart rejoiced,
the dust fills like a crack in the earth."
"Woe!" he spake and cast himself into the dust,
"Woe!" spake Gilgamesh and cast himself into the dust.

(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see the man with one son?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. A peg is fixed in his wall, and he weepeth bitterly over it."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see the man with two sons?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. He sitteth upon two bricks and eateth a loaf of bread."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see the man with three sons?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. He drinketh water from a skin that hangeth at the saddle."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see the man with four sons?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. Like the master of a team of asses his heart rejoiceth."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see the man with five sons?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. Like a good scribe his hand is deft, with ease he entereth the palace."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see the man with six sons?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. Like a ploughman his heart rejoiceth."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see the man with seven sons?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. Among the young gods he sitteth upon a throne and heareth the council."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see the eunuch of the palace?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. Like a fair standard he leaneth in the corner."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see him that a mooring pole struck down?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. Woe to his mother and his father! When the pegs are pulled out he wandereth about."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see him that died his own death?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. He lieth upon the bed of the gods and drinketh clear water."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see him that fell in battle?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. His father and his mother honour his memory, and his wife weepeth over him."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see him whose corpse was left lying in the wild?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. His ghost findeth no rest in the netherworld."
(Gilgamesh:) "Didst thou see him whose ghost hath none to bring him offerings for the dead?"
(Enkidu:) "I saw him. He eateth the scrapings from the pot and the crusts of bread that are cast into the lane."
