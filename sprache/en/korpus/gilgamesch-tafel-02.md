Enkidu sat before her, the harlot,
the two of them made love together,
and he forgot the wild where he was born.
The harlot opened her mouth and spake unto Enkidu:
(Šamḫat:) "I look upon thee, Enkidu, like unto a god art thou,
why dost thou roam the wild with the beasts?
Come, I will lead thee to Uruk, the broad city,
to the pure house, the dwelling of Anu.
Arise, Enkidu, I will bring thee to Eanna,
where folk ply works of skill,
and thou also, a true man, shalt make thy place there.
Thou art torn away now from the lands that have no shepherd."

He heard her word and gave consent,
the counsel of the woman fell into his heart.
One garment she put on herself,
with the second garment she clothed him.
She took him by the hand and led him like a god
to the camp of the shepherds, where the fold stood.
The shepherds gathered about him
and spake among themselves:
(Shepherds:) "This man, how like he is in form to Gilgamesh,
tall of growth is he, towering like a battlement!
Surely he was born in the hills,
the milk of the beasts hath he sucked,
his strength is mighty as a lump from the heaven of Anu."

They set bread before him,
they set beer before him.
Enkidu ate not of the bread, he stared and peered,
to eat bread he had not been taught,
to drink beer Enkidu knew not.
The harlot opened her mouth and spake unto Enkidu:
(Šamḫat:) "Eat the bread, Enkidu, it is the due of life,
drink the beer, so is it decreed for the land!"
Enkidu ate the bread until he was full,
beer he drank, seven tankards full.
Then was his spirit loosed, he sang,
his heart rejoiced, and his face shone.

The barber shaved his hairy body,
he anointed himself with oil and became a man,
he put on a garment and was like a warrior,
he took his weapon to fight with the lions.
When the shepherds lay down at night,
he slew the wolves and chased away the lions.
The old shepherds slept,
Enkidu was their watchman, a man that waketh.

A man was bidden to a wedding,
while Enkidu took his pleasure with Šamḫat.
He lifted his eyes and saw the man,
he spake unto the harlot:
(Enkidu:) "Šamḫat, fetch me the man hither,
why he came, I would hear his speech."
The harlot called the man,
she went to him and spake with him:
(Šamḫat:) "Whither hastenest thou, man,
what is this toilsome going?"
The man opened his mouth and spake unto Enkidu:
(Guest:) "To the wedding house they have bidden me,
such is the lot of folk, to choose a bride.
The feasting table will I load
with dainty meats for the wedding house.
For the king of Uruk, the broad city,
the veil is lifted before him that chooseth first,
for Gilgamesh, the king of Uruk, the broad city,
the veil is lifted before him that chooseth first.
He lieth with the destined wife,
he first, the husband after.
By the counsel of the gods it is so spoken,
when they cut his navel cord, she was decreed for him."
At the words of the man Enkidu's face grew pale.

Enkidu went before, and Šamḫat followed him.
He entered Uruk, the broad city,
and the throng gathered about him.
He stepped into the lane of Uruk the Fold,
the folk assembled and talked of him:
(People:) "In form he is like Gilgamesh,
smaller of growth, but stronger of bone.
Surely he was born in the hills,
the milk of the beasts hath he sucked."
The land of Uruk stood about him,
the land was gathered about him,
the throng pressed before him,
the men crowded about him,
as to a little child they kissed his feet.

For Išḫara the bed was made ready,
for Gilgamesh, like unto a god, a rival was set.
Enkidu barred with his foot the door of the wedding house
and let not Gilgamesh enter.
They seized each other at the door of the wedding house,
in the lane they wrestled, on the broad market of the land.
They seized each other and bent like bulls,
the doorposts shook, the wall trembled.
Gilgamesh and Enkidu seized each other and bent like bulls,
the doorposts shook, the wall trembled.
Gilgamesh bent his knee, his foot upon the ground,
then his wrath was stilled, and he let go.
When he had let go,
Enkidu spake unto him, unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "As the only one did thy mother bear thee,
the Wild Cow of the Fold, Ninsun.
Exalted is thy head above the warriors,
the kingship over mankind hath Enlil decreed thee."

(Gilgamesh:) "The strongest in the land is he, strength hath he,
his strength is mighty as a lump from the heaven of Anu,
tall of growth is he, towering like a battlement."
The mother of Gilgamesh opened her mouth and spake unto her son,
the Wild Cow Ninsun opened her mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Ninsun:) "My son, bitterly he waileth,
Enkidu hath neither kin nor friend,
his hair hangeth loose,
in the wild was he born, and none is his brother."
Enkidu stood by, he heard her speech,
he pondered it, sat down and wept.
His eyes filled with tears,
his arms grew slack, his strength was gone.
They took hold of each other,
they clasped their hands together.

Gilgamesh spake a word unto Enkidu:
(Gilgamesh:) "Why, my friend, are thine eyes filled with tears,
thine arms grown slack, thy strength gone?"
Enkidu spake unto him, unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, my heart is full of pain,
with sobbing my legs do tremble,
fear is gone into my heart."
Gilgamesh opened his mouth and spake unto Enkidu:
(Gilgamesh:) "Now then, my friend, let us go unto Ḫumbaba,
cedars will I fell,
the whirlwinds shall shatter him."

Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "How shall we go, my friend, unto the Cedar Forest?
To keep the cedars safe
hath Enlil appointed him a terror to mankind.
That road is none for going,
that man is none for beholding.
The warden of the Cedar Forest is strong and never resteth,
Ḫumbaba, his voice is the Flood,
his mouth is fire, his breath is death.
Sixty leagues away he heareth the rustling of his forest,
who is he that goeth down into his forest?
Adad is the first, and he is the second,
who among the Igigi will stand against him?
To keep the cedars safe
hath Enlil appointed him a terror to mankind,
and whoso goeth down into his forest, weakness seizeth him."

Gilgamesh spake unto him, unto Enkidu:
(Gilgamesh:) "Why, my friend, speakest thou like a weakling?
With thy slack mouth thou hast grieved my heart.
Man, his days are numbered,
whatsoever he doeth is but wind.
Thou wast born and didst grow up in the wild,
lions leapt upon thee, thou hast known all things,
and men also fled before thee.
Thy heart is tried, battle hast thou seen.
Come, my friend, let us go to the forge!"
To the forge they went,
they sat down and took counsel together:
(Gilgamesh:) "Let us cast axes,
hatchets of seven talents,
swords of seven talents,
belts of one talent each."

(Gilgamesh:) "Hear me, ye young men of Uruk the Fold,
ye young men of Uruk that understand battle!
I am grown so bold as to go the far road to Ḫumbaba.
A battle I know not shall I face,
a road I know not shall I ride.
Bless me, that I may go,
that I may see your faces again in safety
and enter glad of heart through the gate of Uruk.
I will come home and hold the Akītu feast twice in the year,
hold will I the Akītu twice in the year.
Let the Akītu be kept and the rejoicing begin,
let the drums roar before the Wild Cow Ninsun!"

Enkidu gave counsel to the elders
and to the young men of Uruk that understand battle:
(Enkidu:) "Tell him he shall not go to the Cedar Forest.
That road is none for going,
that man is none for beholding.
The warden of the Cedar Forest is strong and never resteth,
Ḫumbaba, his voice is the Flood,
his mouth is fire, his breath is death.
Sixty leagues away he heareth the rustling of his forest,
who is he that goeth down into his forest?
Adad is the first, and he is the second,
who among the Igigi will stand against him?
To keep the cedars safe
hath Enlil appointed him a terror to mankind,
and whoso goeth down into his forest, weakness seizeth him."

The great counsellors arose
and gave answer unto Gilgamesh:
(Counsellors:) "Young art thou, Gilgamesh, thy heart carrieth thee away,
and what thou speakest of thou understandest not.
Ḫumbaba, his voice is the Flood,
his mouth is fire, his breath is death.
Sixty leagues away he heareth the rustling of his forest,
whoso goeth down into his forest, weakness seizeth him.
Who is he that goeth down into his forest?
Who among the Igigi will stand against him?
Adad is the first, and he is the second.
To keep the cedars safe
hath Enlil appointed him a terror to mankind."
Gilgamesh heard the words of the great counsellors,
he looked up and gazed upon Enkidu.
