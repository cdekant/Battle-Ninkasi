(Revellers:) "Gakkul, Gakkul, thou fat paunch,
Gakkul, and thou, Lamdi, slender neck,
Gakkul, thou liftest up our spirits,
Lamdi, thou makest our hearts soft,
Ugurbala, thou ornament of the house, whom no maid ever dusteth,
Šagube, thou pourest and askest not,
Amam, thou haulest in what the Lamdi thirsteth for,
Buniĝ of reed and Bandudu, ye buckets for all manner of leftovers,
ye stout vessels, ranged upon the rack like maidens before the round dance,
and may thy god at last be appeased!
What gleameth in the eye of the Gakkul gleameth soon in our eyes,
what fermenteth in the belly of the Gakkul fermenteth soon in our bellies.
What filleth thee within
filleth us also, and thoroughly.
Our spirits are risen, our hearts are grown soft.
Upon thy foundation stone was water poured, thy house standeth firm,
so may Ninkasi take up her dwelling with thee and never depart,
may she bring beer and wine and no man pay,
may juice and honey call out for thee, loudly, all the night long!"

(Master of the house:) "In the Buniĝ of reed standeth sweet beer,
the young cupbearer bringeth me sweet-smelling things and smelleth not ill himself.
When I walk around the basin,
when I fill myself and fill myself again,
when the beer striketh me dumb
and the juice driveth me to gladness,
when my heart exulteth and my spirit riseth,
when I clothe the joy I carry in my heart
in a festive robe and take it out walking,
then Inana is whole again!"

(Woman:) "So the queen of heaven is whole again. And so am I."
