They stood and marvelled at the forest,
they gazed up at the lofty cedars,
they gazed upon the entrance of the forest.
Where Ḫumbaba went about there was a track,
the paths were straight, and good was the way.
They beheld the Cedar Mountain,
the dwelling of the gods, the throne of the goddesses,
before the mountain the cedar bore its plenty,
sweet was its shade, full of delight.
Thick tangled was the thorn, the forest a shrouding roof,
cedar and balsam, twined together, left no way in.
A league around the cedar put forth its saplings,
the cypress two thirds of a league.
Crusted with resin the cedars grew sixty cubits high,
the resin welled forth and trickled like rain,
it flowed away, and the ravines bore it off.

Through all the forest a bird began to sing,
the hens gave answer, a ceaseless din was the noise.
A lonely cricket struck up a noisy song,
they sang and made the pipe resound.
The wood pigeon cooeth, the turtle dove calleth in answer,
at the call of the stork the forest exulteth,
at the cry of the francolin the forest exulteth in its plenty.
The monkey mothers sing aloud, the monkey young one shrieketh,
like a band of minstrels and drummers
they beat the measure day by day before Ḫumbaba.

When the cedar cast its shadow,
fear fell upon Gilgamesh,
stiffness gripped his arms,
and trembling seized his knees.
Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "Let us go down deep into the forest,
let us stir our hands and raise a loud cry!"
Gilgamesh opened his mouth and spake unto Enkidu:
(Gilgamesh:) "Why, my friend, do we tremble like cowards,
we that have crossed all the mountains?
My friend is tried in battle,
he that hath seen the fight feareth not death.
With blood thou art spattered, so fear not death,
fall into frenzy like one possessed, let thy mind be changed!
Like a kettledrum let thy cry resound,
let the stiffness leave thine arms, the trembling thy knees!
Take my hand, friend, together let us go,
let thy heart think upon battle!
Forget death and seek life!
He that walketh at the side is the prudent man,
he that goeth before shall guard himself and keep his comrade whole.
It is they that made a name for days far off!"
They came both of them into the distance,
they ceased their talking and stood still.

Ḫumbaba spake a word within himself:
(Ḫumbaba:) "Surely it is Enkidu,
may Enlil curse him!"
Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, one friend alone is one, but two are two!
Though they be weak, two they are still,
can one alone not climb the steep rampart, two shall do it!
A rope of three strands breaketh not lightly,
two whelps overcome the mighty hound.
Set thy feet firm upon the ground!
His back is a storm, his brow is wind,
speak unto Šamaš, that he give thee his winds!"
Gilgamesh lifted his head and wept before Šamaš,
in the glare of the sun his tears did flow:
(Gilgamesh:) "Forget not the day that thou spakest unto me in Uruk!
Now stand by me,
over Gilgamesh, the offspring of Uruk's midst, spread thy shadow!"
Šamaš heard what his mouth had spoken,
forthwith a voice cried out from heaven:
(Voice:) "Fear not, stand against him, he shall not go into his forest,
he shall not go down into the grove, he shall not put on his terrible radiance,
he shall not wrap himself in his seven cloaks!
In one he is wrapped, six he hath cast off."

Like a savage bull, ready to gore,
he bellowed once, a bellow full of terror,
the warden of the forests bellowed,
Ḫumbaba thundered like the god of storms.
Ḫumbaba opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Ḫumbaba:) "Let fools, Gilgamesh, take counsel with a dolt! Why art thou come before me?
Come, Enkidu, thou spawn of fish, that knewest not thy father,
hatchling of turtle and terrapin, that suckedst not the milk of thy mother!
When thou wast little I watched thee, but I came not near thee.
Now in guile thou bringest Gilgamesh before me
and standest here like a stranger foe?
I will cut the gullet and the neck of Gilgamesh,
I will feed his flesh to the birds of the forest, the screaming eagle and the vulture!"

Gilgamesh opened his mouth and spake unto Enkidu:
(Gilgamesh:) "My friend, the face of Ḫumbaba is changed!
Boldly we came up unto his lair to overcome him,
but the heart that took fright groweth not still in a moment."
Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "Why, my friend, speakest thou like a weakling?
With thy slack mouth thou grievest my heart.
Now, my friend, but one thing is to be done,
already the copper floweth into the mould!
Stoke the furnace an hour, blow the coals an hour?
To send the Flood is to crack the whip!
Draw not back thy foot, turn not behind thee,
make thy blow mighty like a lion!
Keep in thy mind all the dreams that thou hast seen!"

They smote the ground, that they fell into the deep,
he lifted them up, but the sky was too far,
he smote the earth, but the rock withstood him.
Beneath their heels the earth was split,
as they circled each other Sirara and Lebanon were rent.
The white cloud turned black,
death rained upon them like a mist.
Šamaš roused against Ḫumbaba the great storms:
South Wind, North Wind, East Wind, West Wind, gust,
countergust, weather, foul weather, whirlwind,
wind of doom, ice wind, hurricane and waterspout:
thirteen winds arose, and the face of Ḫumbaba was darkened,
he cannot thrust forward, he cannot kick backward,
and the weapons of Gilgamesh reached Ḫumbaba.

Ḫumbaba pleaded for his life and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Ḫumbaba:) "Young art thou, Gilgamesh, as when thy mother bore thee,
yet thou art the offspring of the Wild Cow Ninsun!
At the word of Šamaš thou hast levelled ten mountains,
thou offspring of Uruk's midst, King Gilgamesh!
Never, Gilgamesh, did a dead man please his master,
but a living servant serveth his lord.
Gilgamesh, spare my life!
Let me stay here in the forest and be at thy service,
trees will I keep for thee, as many as thou askest,
myrtle, cedar and cypress will I keep for thee,
timber to be the adornment of thy palace!"
Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "Hearken not, my friend, to the pleading of Ḫumbaba!
If he return home, we are as the unborn,
he will bind us fast in the Cedar Forest."

Ḫumbaba lifted his head and wept before Šamaš,
in the glare of the sun his tears did flow:
(Ḫumbaba:) "Thou knowest the ways of my forest, Enkidu,
and thou knowest all that is to be said.
Had I but lifted thee up and hanged thee from a sapling at the entrance of my forest,
had I but fed thy flesh to the birds of the forest, the screaming eagle and the vulture!
Now, Enkidu, my release lieth with thee,
speak unto Gilgamesh, that he spare my life!"
Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, Ḫumbaba, the warden of the Cedar Forest,
make an end of him, slay him, blot out his might,
before Enlil, the foremost, heareth of it!
The great gods might be wroth with us,
Enlil in Nippur, Šamaš in Larsa.
Set up for ever a fame that endureth:
how Gilgamesh slew Ḫumbaba!"
Twice more Ḫumbaba pleaded, twice more Enkidu spake against him,
then Ḫumbaba lifted his head and wept before Šamaš:
(Ḫumbaba:) "May they not grow old, the two of them,
save his friend Gilgamesh may Enkidu have none to bury him!"
Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, I speak unto thee, but thou hearest me not!
Let the curses return into his mouth."

Gilgamesh heard the words of his friend,
he drew the dagger at his side.
Gilgamesh smote him in the neck,
Enkidu tore out his heart, drew it forth with the lungs.
From the head he took the tusks as spoil,
blood in plenty fell upon the mountain,
blood in plenty fell upon the mountain.

Gilgamesh went trampling through the forest,
the resin of the cedars he fetched for the table of Enlil.
Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, we have made the forest a waste,
how shall we answer Enlil in Nippur?
'In your might ye slew the warden,
what wrath was this, that ye trampled the forest?'"
When they had slain his seven sons,
Cricket, Screecher, Whirlwind, Shrieker, Cunning and Storm,
hatchets of two talents were their axes,
three cubits and a half long were the chips of every stroke.
Gilgamesh felled the trees,
and Enkidu sought out the finest timber.

Enkidu opened his mouth and spake unto Gilgamesh:
(Enkidu:) "My friend, we have felled a lofty cedar,
whose crown touched the heavens.
I made a door, six rods high, two rods broad, one cubit thick,
hinge, pivot and sill are all of one piece.
To the house of Enlil in Nippur the Euphrates shall bear it,
let the sanctuary of Nippur rejoice!"
Of logs of cedar and cypress they joined a raft,
they bound the raft and laid the cedars upon it.
Enkidu rode upon it,
and Gilgamesh rode with him, bearing the head of Ḫumbaba.
