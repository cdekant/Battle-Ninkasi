Unto Kurnugi, the land whence none cometh home,
did Ištar, the daughter of Sîn, turn her mind,
yea, the daughter of Sîn turned thither her mind:
unto the darksome house, the seat of Irkalla,
unto the house whence none goeth forth that went therein,
unto the path whose going knoweth no turning,
unto the house whose guests are bereft of light,
where dust is their meal and clay their bread.
Light they behold never, in darkness they crouch,
feathered as birds, with wings for their raiment,
upon door and bolt the dust lieth heaped,
over the lintel the stillness of death is poured.

When Ištar was come unto the gate of Kurnugi,
she opened her mouth and began to speak,
unto the keeper of the gate she spake the word:
(Ištar:) "Keeper, ho! Open me thy gate!
Open thy gate, that I may enter in!
If thou openest not the gate, that I may enter in,
I shall smite the door and shatter the bolt,
I shall smite the posts and overthrow the leaves,
I shall shatter the threshold and rend away the knob,
I shall raise up the dead, that they devour the living,
more than the living shall the dead be!"

The keeper opened his mouth and began to speak,
he spake unto the great one, unto Ištar:
(Keeper:) "Hold, my lady, cast it not down!
Tarry here within, the door shall be opened.
Let me go and make known thy name unto Ereškigal the queen."
The keeper went in and spake unto Ereškigal:
(Keeper:) "Behold, thy sister Ištar standeth at thy gate,
she that holdeth the great cords of play, she that stirreth the deep before Ea the king."

When Ereškigal heard this,
her face grew wan as a felled tamarisk,
her lips grew black as the rim of a vat daubed with pitch.
(Ereškigal:) "What drove her heart unto me, what hath stirred her spirit against me?
See now: I drink water with the Anunnaki,
clay I eat for bread, troubled water I drink for beer.
I will weep for the men that forsook their wives,
weep for the maidens torn from the lap of their lovers,
and for the tender babe will I weep, that was driven forth before his time.
Go, keeper, open unto her thy gate
and deal with her after the ancient ordinances!"

The keeper went and opened unto her his gate:
(Keeper:) "Enter, my lady, that Kutha may rejoice over thee,
that the palace of Kurnugi may be glad before thy face!"

Through the first gate he let her in, he loosed and took from her head the great crown.
(Ištar:) "Wherefore, keeper, hast thou taken from my head the great crown?"
(Keeper:) "Enter, my lady, such are the ordinances of the mistress of the earth."

Through the second gate he let her in, he loosed and took from her ears the pendants.
(Ištar:) "Wherefore, keeper, hast thou taken from my ears the pendants?"
(Keeper:) "Enter, my lady, such are the ordinances of the mistress of the earth."

Through the third gate he let her in, he loosed and took from her neck the strings of beads.
(Ištar:) "Wherefore, keeper, hast thou taken from my neck the strings of beads?"
(Keeper:) "Enter, my lady, such are the ordinances of the mistress of the earth."

Through the fourth gate he let her in, he loosed and took from her breast the pins of her garment.
(Ištar:) "Wherefore, keeper, hast thou taken from my breast the pins of my garment?"
(Keeper:) "Enter, my lady, such are the ordinances of the mistress of the earth."

Through the fifth gate he let her in, he loosed and took from her hips the girdle of birthstones.
(Ištar:) "Wherefore, keeper, hast thou taken from my hips the girdle of birthstones?"
(Keeper:) "Enter, my lady, such are the ordinances of the mistress of the earth."

Through the sixth gate he let her in, he loosed and took from her hands and feet the bangles.
(Ištar:) "Wherefore, keeper, hast thou taken from my hands and feet the bangles?"
(Keeper:) "Enter, my lady, such are the ordinances of the mistress of the earth."

Through the seventh gate he let her in, he loosed and took from her body the garment of her dignity.
(Ištar:) "Wherefore, keeper, hast thou taken from my body the garment of my dignity?"
(Keeper:) "Enter, my lady, such are the ordinances of the mistress of the earth."

No sooner was Ištar gone down to Kurnugi,
than Ereškigal saw her and trembled with wrath before her face.
Ištar took no counsel and sat herself above her.
Ereškigal opened her mouth and began to speak,
unto Namtar, her messenger, she spake the word:
(Ereškigal:) "Go, Namtar, step before me,
loose sixty plagues upon her, let them lay hold on Ištar:
sickness of the eyes into her eyes,
sickness of the side into her sides,
sickness of the feet into her feet,
sickness of the heart into her heart,
sickness of the head into her head,
into her wholly, into her within!"

After the lady Ištar was gone down to Kurnugi,
the bull mounted the cow no more, the ass covered not the she-ass,
the lad in the lane got the maid with child no more,
the lad slept in his chamber,
the maid slept all alone.

Papsukkal, messenger of the great gods, hung his nose, his face was dark,
clad in mourning he went, with tangled hair,
went in sorrow and wept before Sîn his father,
before Ea the king his tears did flow:
(Papsukkal:) "Ištar is gone down unto the earth and hath not come up again.
Since Ištar went down to Kurnugi,
the bull mounteth the cow no more, the ass covereth not the she-ass,
the lad in the lane getteth the maid with child no more,
the lad sleepeth in his chamber,
the maid sleepeth all alone."

Ea devised in the wisdom of his heart a counsel,
he made Aṣûšu-namir, the assinnu.
(Ea:) "Up, Aṣûšu-namir, set thy face toward the gate of Kurnugi,
the seven gates of Kurnugi shall open before thee,
Ereškigal shall see thee and rejoice at the sight of thee.
When her heart is soothed and her spirit made bright,
then make her swear by the life of the great gods.
Lift up thy head, set thy mind upon the waterskin:
'Ah, my lady, let them give me the skin, that I may drink water therefrom.'"

When Ereškigal heard this,
she smote her thigh and bit her finger:
(Ereškigal:) "Thou hast asked of me what none may ask!
Up, Aṣûšu-namir, I curse thee with a great curse,
a lot will I decree thee that shall not pass away for ever:
what lieth in the furrows before the city be thy bread,
the gutters of the city be thy place of drinking,
the shadow of the wall be thy standing place,
the threshold be thy seat,
the drunken and the thirsty shall smite thee upon the cheek!"

Ereškigal opened her mouth and began to speak,
unto Namtar, her messenger, she spake the word:
(Ereškigal:) "Go, Namtar, knock upon the Egalgina,
adorn the thresholds with shells,
lead forth the Anunnaki and set them upon thrones of gold,
sprinkle Ištar with the water of life and bring her before me."
Namtar went and knocked upon the Egalgina,
adorned the thresholds with shells,
led forth the Anunnaki and set them upon thrones of gold,
sprinkled Ištar with the water of life and brought her before her.
(Ereškigal:) "Go, Namtar: if she give thee not the ransom, bring her hither again."

Through the first gate he led her out and gave her back the garment of her dignity,
through the second gate he led her out and gave her back the bangles of hands and feet,
through the third gate he led her out and gave her back the girdle of birthstones,
through the fourth gate he led her out and gave her back the pins of her breast,
through the fifth gate he led her out and gave her back the strings of beads of her neck,
through the sixth gate he led her out and gave her back the pendants of her ears,
through the seventh gate he led her out and gave her back the great crown of her head.

"If she give thee not the ransom, bring her hither again.
And Dumuzi, the beloved of her youth,
wash with pure water, anoint with sweet oil,
clothe him in a red garment, let him play the flute of lapis lazuli,
let harlots turn his spirit."

Belili set her jewels in order,
with eyestones was her lap filled.
When she heard the wailing for her brother, Belili shattered her jewels,
the eyestones that filled the face of the wild cow.
(Belili:) "Rob me not of my only brother!
On the day that Dumuzi cometh up, with him come up the flute of lapis lazuli and the ring of carnelian,
with him come up the wailing men and the wailing women,
let the dead come up and smell the incense!"
