At the first glimmer of dawn
Gilgamesh lamented for his friend:
(Gilgamesh:) "Enkidu, whom thy mother, a gazelle,
and thy father, a wild ass, begot,
whom the wild she-asses nursed with their milk
and the beasts of the wild taught all the pastures!
The paths of the Cedar Forest, Enkidu,
shall weep for thee without rest, by day and by night!
The elders of the teeming city of Uruk the Fold shall weep for thee!
The throng that blessed us as we went shall weep for thee!
The high peaks of hills and mountains shall weep for thee,
the pastures shall lament like thy mother!
Boxwood, cypress and cedar shall weep for thee,
through whose midst we crept in our fury!
The bear shall weep for thee, the hyena, the panther, the cheetah, the stag and the jackal,
the lion, the wild bull, the deer, the ibex, the herds and the beasts of the wild!
The holy river Ulāy shall weep for thee, along whose banks we walked in our strength!
The pure Euphrates shall weep for thee,
whose water we poured from skins in libation!
The young men of Uruk the Fold shall weep for thee,
who saw our battle when we slew the Bull of Heaven!
The ploughman shall weep for thee in his furrow,
who praiseth thy name with his sweet work song!
The shepherd shall weep for thee in his pen,
who made sweet for thy mouth the milk, the beer and the mixed beer!
The shepherd boy shall weep for thee,
who spread ghee upon thy lips!
The brewer shall weep for thee,
who gave fine beer into thy mouth!
The harlot shall weep for thee,
who anointed thy crown with fragrant oil!
They shall weep for thee as brothers,
they shall loose their hair down their backs as sisters!
On this day will I myself weep for thee."

(Gilgamesh:) "Hear me, ye young men, hear me!
Hear me, ye elders of the teeming city of Uruk, hear me!
I will weep for Enkidu, my friend,
like a wailing woman will I lament bitterly.
The axe at my side, in which my arm trusted,
the sword at my belt, the shield before me,
my festive garment, the girdle of my joy:
an evil wind hath risen against me and robbed me.
My friend, fleeing mule, wild ass of the mountains, panther of the wild,
my Enkidu, fleeing mule, wild ass of the mountains, panther of the wild!
It was we that joined together and climbed the mountains,
seized the Bull of Heaven and slew it,
destroyed Ḫumbaba, who dwelt in the Cedar Forest.
What sleep is this that hath seized thee now?
Thou art grown dark and hearest me not!"
But he lifted not his head,
Gilgamesh felt his heart, but it beat no more.
He veiled the face of his friend like a bride,
he circled about him like an eagle,
like a lioness robbed of her cubs
he turned this way and that.
He tore his curling hair and cast it in heaps,
he rent off his finery and flung it away like a thing forbidden.

At the first glimmer of dawn
Gilgamesh sent forth a call through the land:
(Gilgamesh:) "Smith, lapidary, coppersmith, goldsmith, jeweller!
Make me an image of my friend!"
He made an image of his friend, such as none before:
(Gilgamesh:) "Thy brows shall be of lapis lazuli, thy breast of gold."
He tore his curling hair and cast it in heaps,
he rent off his finery and flung it away like a thing forbidden.
(Gilgamesh:) "I will lay thee upon a great bed,
upon a bed of honour will I lay thee,
I will set thee upon a quiet seat, the seat at my left,
the princes of the earth shall kiss thy feet.
I will make the folk of Uruk weep and sob for thee,
the joyful folk will I fill with grief for thee.
And I, when thou art gone, will wear the matted hair of mourning,
I will wrap myself in a lion's skin and roam the wild."

At the first glimmer of dawn
he opened the treasury, loosed the seals and looked upon the jewels:
obsidian, carnelian and alabaster,
gold by minas and by talents, ivory, weapons with hilts of gold,
all this he gave unto his friend.
He ceased to sob for his friend,
fat oxen and fatted sheep he slaughtered and heaped them up for his friend:
(Gilgamesh:) "These gifts, Šamaš, I show forth for my friend!"
A throwstick of dark wood, the gleaming wood,
he showed for Ištar, the great queen, before Šamaš:
(Gilgamesh:) "Let Ištar, the great queen, receive this,
let her welcome my friend and walk at his side!"
So he showed before Šamaš the gifts for the gods of the netherworld:
a robe for Bēlet-ilī, a flask of lapis lazuli for Ereškigal,
a flute of carnelian for Dumuzi, the shepherd whom Ištar loveth,
a throne and a staff of lapis lazuli for Namtar, the messenger of the netherworld,
clasp and bangles for Qāssa-ṭābat, the sweeper of Ereškigal,
a casket of alabaster with an image of the Cedar Forest for Ninšuluḫḫatumma, who cleanseth the house,
a two-edged dagger for Bibbu, the butcher of the netherworld,
a flask of alabaster for Dumuzi-abzu, the scapegoat of the netherworld,
and at every gift he spake:
(Gilgamesh:) "Let this be received, let my friend be welcomed and walked beside,
let my friend not lose heart nor grow sick within!"

When Gilgamesh heard this,
he devised in his heart the damming of the river.
At the first glimmer of dawn
Gilgamesh opened his gate.
He brought out a great table of elammaku wood,
he filled a bowl of carnelian with syrup,
he filled a bowl of lapis lazuli with ghee,
he adorned the table and showed it unto Šamaš.
